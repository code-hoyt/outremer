# Outremer — Playable races research

*Research for Q9, written 2026-11-11. Nothing here is locked except what DESIGN.md already records. Names are working titles.*

**Already locked:** four playable races (Humans plus three), with the Redeemed (turned profane beings, story-unlocked) as a possible fifth later. **Peri** is one of the three: small, quick, faint wings that give a short hop; Persian legend; scouts, healers, archers. Cletus does not want strictly Biblical races (so Nephilim and Anakim are out). Some classes will be race-locked, FFTA2-style; how strict that is (Q11), whether non-Humans join freely (Q12), and whether races also change stats or movement (Q13) are still open. Q14 (can playable races fall, vs the profane enemy kinds) is also open.

**Art constraint that should drive every pick:** units are drawn up to 60 art px in a 64×68 cell. On a phone the integer scale is 3. A race that only reads as "a human with ears" will vanish in a cluster. Head shape, body mass, and limb plan have to be obvious at that size. Stay bipedal if possible; a centaur or a full bull-bodied lamassu would fight the cell and the feet anchor.

If Q13 later says races do not change movement, bake the movement notes below into race-locked classes rather than into the race itself. Do not invent a transform gauge or a "press to change form" step: that is a stop-and-press system, and Fire Emblem's Laguz show how easily it becomes a chore.

## 1. What other games do

**Final Fantasy Tactics Advance / A2.** The gold standard for this project. FFTA has five races (Hume, Bangaa, Viera, Moogle, Nu Mou); A2 adds Seeq and Gria. Identity is mostly the **class list**, plus a readable silhouette and a broad role: Humes are the wide generalists; Bangaa are lizard-like melee and durable; Nu Mou are the dedicated mages (and beast-masters); Viera are fast, slender, stealth and archery; Moogles are small, frail up close, strong at range and gadgets; Seeq are heavy mixed fighters with a short list; Gria fly and also have a short list. Movement itself is usually job-based, not race-based, except where a race is built around one trick (Gria flight). The lesson: race-locked jobs work when each race has a clear job and a look you can pick off a 64px sprite. The warning: Seeq and Gria feel thin because they have too few jobs. If a race is only four classes, it needs a very strong movement or battlefield trick, or it will feel like a novelty.

**Tactics Ogre.** Humans carry almost the whole generic tree. Winged folk are nearly human, but they fly and get the exclusive Vartan class. Lizards, lamia and orcs share a short demi-human tree (Hoplite, Juggernaut, Patriarch). Faeries and other marvels are more like specialist recruits than full class trees. The lesson: one "human-plus" race with a movement hook (flight) can work, but only if the silhouette or the movement is unmistakable. Do not make a second Peri.

**Fire Emblem (Laguz, Manakete, Taguel).** Beast, bird and dragon tribes, plus stone-triggered dragon and rabbit shifters. Silhouettes are excellent in beast form. The gameplay hook is a transform gauge or a dragonstone: weak or unable to attack until you shift, strong afterwards. That is a resource mini-game on top of the turn, and it is the opposite of "the system comes out of choices you are already making." Take the silhouettes and the tribal cultures; leave the gauge.

**Unicorn Overlord.** Humans hold the generic tree. Elves, Bestrals (beast-folk) and angels (winged folk of Albion) have their own classes. Angels fly and often strengthen in daylight; beast-folk strengthen at night; elves mix physical and magical hits. Day/night and flight are identities you feel without a menu. Elves are the weak lesson: they can read as "pretty humans" if the ears and proportions are timid.

**Divinity: Original Sin 2.** Five options (Human, Elf, Dwarf, Lizard, plus Undead as a modifier of the other four). Each race has a unique skill and a couple of passives, but **classes are not race-locked**. Elves eat memories from corpses; lizards breathe fire and shrug off heat; undead heal from poison and are harmed by ordinary healing. The Undead-as-modifier is a useful thought for the Redeemed later: a fallen or returned state on top of a base people, not a whole new body plan. The lesson for us: a race needs one verb you can see, not only a stat tweak. We should not copy the "any class on any race" structure, because the FFTA2 locks are already the plan.

**Dragon Age.** Elves, dwarves, Qunari, humans. The Qunari work because of **horns and mass**, plus a culture (the Qun) that is not "generic warrior tribe." Dwarves have a hard hook (they cannot be mages). Elves often slip toward "slender human." Culture and one hard limit beat a list of minor stat bonuses.

**The Elder Scrolls (Khajiit, Argonian).** These endure because the cultures are real (moons and moon-sugar; the Hist and the swamp), not because they are "cat people" and "lizard people." Khajiit also come in several body plans, from housecat-sized to tiger-like, which is a luxury we cannot afford across four races, but it is a reminder that beast-folk need a society, not just a head swap. At 60 px, a shy pair of ears will not carry a race; a muzzle, a tail mass, or a wholly different stature will.

**Pathfinder / D&D standouts.** Tieflings and goliaths read at a glance (horns and tail; stone-mottled size). Genasi are the tabletop cousin of djinn-blooded: elemental people, but they need a strong crown, hair, or skin tell or they become "humans with a tint." Kenku have a bird head and often cannot fly, which is a better identity than "bird person who also flies" when Peri already has the hop. Tabaxi and aasimar are the cautionary cases: cat ears, or a faint glow, disappear at our scale. Yuan-ti / naga-style serpent bodies, leonin, and loxodon (elephant-folk) have stronger outlines; loxodon would be distinctive, but the setting fit is thinner than a Near Eastern lion or bull.

**Wildermyth.** No playable races. Heroes are human, and they change shape through scars, grafts and mystic interfusion as the story happens. That is closer to our veteran traits and rare recruit-only traits than to a race list. It is a good reminder that some of the "this unit is special" feeling can come from history, not from ancestry.

**Other useful comparisons.** *The Banner Saga* gives you humans, Varl (huge horned giants) and Horseborn (centaur-folk). Three or four types, huge size contrast, and the Horseborn's body plan is a movement identity. Varl are the model for "one tall, heavy people." *Battle for Wesnoth* makes nagas and merfolk matter because they treat water as home and land as a tax. *Fell Seal* and *Triangle Strategy* do almost all of their variety with classes and named characters, which works, but it is not the FFTA2 feeling Cletus asked for.

**Design lessons to keep:**

- Four races is a good number if each one has a job, a look, and (if Q13 allows it) a movement tell. A fifth Redeemed can be a state, like DOS2 Undead, rather than a fifth anatomy.
- Silhouette first: size, horns, muzzle, tail, wings, or a cloak-shape that is not a human outline. "Human with ears" fails at 60 px.
- Give each race a battlefield verb (hop, glide, slither, ignore sand, hold a gate, see in smoke) that comes out of moving and fighting, not a transform button.
- Do not starve a race of classes. If the list is short, the movement trick must be excellent.
- Culture and moral range matter. A race that is only "the holy ones" or "the savage ones" will fight the story and, in this setting, can slide into real-world caricature.
- Leave true flight, if any, to at most one people, and do not let it copy Peri's short hop.

## 2. Mythology and folklore survey

Favour Silk Road, Mediterranean and Near East-adjacent beings, because the other world should feel like a medieval person in Outremer might have believed it. The Crusades-era "marvels of the East" and Prester John letters are unusually fair game: they are how 12th- and 13th-century writers already imagined other peoples. Wildcards are included, then set down if they clash with Peri or with living religion.

**Persian / Zoroastrian.** Peri are already in (later Persian folklore made them beautiful, often wing-bearing fairies; older Avestan *pairikā* were hostile witches). That later "doing good to be restored" flavour rhymes loosely with the Redeemed, but should not be turned into backstory unless we choose it later. **Div** (daevas) are better as profane enemies or as a path into the Redeemed, not a starting playable race. **Simurgh** (Avestan *Saēna*) is a great composite bird of healing and protection; Sasanian art often gives it a dog-like head, lion limbs and peacock wings. **Huma** is a later fortune-bird and is too close to Peri and Simurgh to be a third winged people.

**Arabic / Islamic folklore.** **Jinn** are not just "genies." In living Islamic belief they are created beings with free will. Folklore then splits them into types: **ifrit** (often fiery and proud), **marid**, **jann**, **si'lah**. **Ghul** should stay an enemy (desert lurer, already in the same family as our Fiends). **Nasnas** (a half-bodied hopper) has a unique movement idea and an unacceptable risk of reading as a disability joke. Playable fire-born people are a strong fit for the deserts around Outremer, but the game should treat them as other-world folklore, not as a depiction of Islamic doctrine, and they should be morally mixed, not "the demons of the other side."

**Mesopotamian.** The land under and beside the Crusader states. **Lamassu / shedu** are human-headed, winged bull or lion guardians of gates and palaces: mass, horns, heavy wings. **Apkallu** are antediluvian sages; the famous type is a bearded human wearing a fish-skin cloak, the fish head crowning his own. There is also a bird-headed winged type. Both are instantly drawable at 60 px and sit naturally next to holy ground (thresholds, purification, civic wisdom).

**Medieval European bestiary and Prester John.** Cynocephali (dog-headed people), blemmyes (faces on their chests), sciapods (one giant foot), panotii (huge ears) were stock "wonders of the East," often placed in India or Ethiopia, and sometimes inside Prester John's imagined Christian kingdom. Crusaders hoped that kingdom would march. **Cynocephali** are the most usable: Saint Christopher is sometimes depicted as dog-headed, which already treats them as people capable of sanctity, not as beasts. Blemmyes and sciapods are unforgettable and also easy to turn into a freak-show or a joke; I would not lead with them. Panotii are "human with ears" taken to a gag.

**Armenian / Georgian / Byzantine, on the doorstep of the setting.** Georgia was a Christian kingdom in this period. **Kaji** are mountain spirits of Kajeti, often smiths and sorcerers, storm-raisers, sometimes hostile, sometimes bound into heroic stories. Folklore can describe them as ugly or hairy; we should not copy that as "comic goblins." Treat them as a craft and mountain people. **Devi** (Georgian ogres, related to Persian div) are better as enemies. **Paskunji** is a helper-bird and overlaps Simurgh. Armenian **vishaps** are storm and water serpent-dragons of lakes and highlands: a Silk Road naga with a closer postal address. Byzantine and Greek bestiaries already knew **centaurs** and **satyrs**. A centaur silhouette is superb and a problem in a 64-wide cell; satyrs risk reading as party fauns unless the horns and hooves are very bold.

**Hindu / Buddhist (Silk Road, use with care).** **Naga** and **garuda** are visually perfect (coil; full bird-warrior) and are still sacred in living religions, and Garuda is a national emblem. Prefer Armenian vishap or Persian simurgh if we want the body plan without standing on a temple image. **Kinnara** (half-bird celestial musicians) overlap Peri. **Yaksha** can be bulky nature guardians but are less distinct at 60 px. **Rakshasa** belong with enemies or the Redeemed.

**Norse, Celtic, Slavic.** Dvergr (craft, stone, underground), jotnar (size), álfar and sidhe (both drift toward "human with slight otherworld tells"), selkies (human until they shift), vila / samodiva (often winged warrior-nymphs: a Slavic Peri). These are playable in another game. Here they are wildcards, and most either overlap Peri or feel imported from a northern campaign.

**Japanese, Chinese, Egyptian, African.** Tengu and kitsune, *huli jing* and dragon-blooded *long* descendants, ba-birds and sphinx-kin, Anansi-kin, the Abada, the Angolan kishi: all interesting, all a long way from Outremer unless someone is a far traveller from a Prester John east. A sphinx-kin would also collide with shedu. Skip as starting races; any one of them could appear later as a unique recruit without becoming a fourth anatomy.

**What I am not recommending as a base race:** Biblical Nephilim or Anakim; generic elves or "Outlander humans with a slight glow" unless Q10 goes that way for the heroes only; Div, ghul, oni, rakshasa and similar as starting playable stocks (enemy or Redeemed material); Laguz-style shifters; nasnas, blemmyes and sciapods as the face of a people.

## 3. Shortlist

Eight candidates to pair with Humans and Peri. All names are placeholders. Movement lines are proposals for if Q13 allows racial movement, or else for a race-locked class.

### Ifriti (working name; also Jann, Nar-born)

- **Origin:** Arabic folklore, the fiery ifrit / desert jann, fictionalised as an other-world people, not as a statement of Islamic belief.
- **Fantasy:** Proud people of smokeless fire, quick-tempered and magical, at home in heat and sand.
- **Silhouette at 60 px:** Human-tall or a little taller; a horned or flame-shaped crown, ember hair, a narrow waist, maybe a heat-haze or smoke trail in motion. No wings (those are Peri's). The horns and hair have to be exaggerated or they will look like tinted Humans.
- **Move / jump:** Ordinary jump. Cheap sand and fire-terrain; perhaps uneasy on holy water or consecrated marble, as a trade-off, if that is not too mean to Q14.
- **Race-locked flavours:** fire duellist; evoker / smoke-caller; desert walker.
- **Setting fit:** The deserts, caravan roads and jinn stories already sit in the same world as Outremer.
- **Sensitivity:** High if handled as "Muslim demons" or wish-granting genies. Keep them morally mixed, other-worldly, and folkloric. Avoid doctrinal claims, possession-as-Islam, and oriental costume pastiche.

### Shedu (working name; lamassu-kin)

- **Origin:** Mesopotamian gate-guardians, human-headed winged bulls or lions.
- **Fantasy:** Tall wardens of thresholds. They were made, or they believe they were made, to stand in doorways and keep something out.
- **Silhouette at 60 px:** The large people. Horned bovine or leonine head-dress, heavy forequarters, hooves, wings worn like a squared cape (stacked, architectural feathers, not Peri's faint vanes). Should dominate a cell without becoming a quadruped.
- **Move / jump:** Low MOV, low JUMP, hard to shove; perhaps they may drop down a height without the usual cost, as a heavy glide.
- **Race-locked flavours:** gate warder; temple guardian; standard-bearer or siege breaker.
- **Setting fit:** Assyria and Babylon are next door to the Crusader states. They also love the holy/profane ground mechanic: a people of doors and precincts.
- **Sensitivity:** Ancient religion, not a living congregation. Avoid turning them into generic "holy cow angels," and do not flatten living Assyrian or Iraqi culture into the design.

### Kaji

- **Origin:** Georgian folklore; smiths and sorcerers of the hidden mountain land Kajeti.
- **Fantasy:** Stocky mountain craft-folk who raise storms and work metal as if it were a language.
- **Silhouette at 60 px:** Shorter than a Human but much broader than a Peri; heavy shoulders, thick limbs, a distinctive cap, braid or smith's apron, perhaps faint spark or spine-like hair. No wings. Mass should read even without a horned head.
- **Move / jump:** Strong on rubble, slope and mountain tiles; poorer on open sand or mud.
- **Race-locked flavours:** smith-warrior; storm-caller; armourer or artificer (a craft class that is not a Moogle joke).
- **Setting fit:** Unusually good. Georgia is a Christian kingdom on the edge of this world, and crusader-era writers knew the Caucasus as a place of marvels. Gives us a heavy people without going Biblical-giant or generic dwarf.
- **Sensitivity:** Living heritage. Do not make them comic ugly goblins, even if some tales do. They are a people of craft and weather, not a punchline.

### Cynocephali (in-world name still needed; sometimes Calystri)

- **Origin:** Medieval bestiary and travel-writing; Prester John; the dog-headed Saint Christopher tradition.
- **Fantasy:** Dog-headed people of the far kingdoms, already present in the way a 13th-century Frank imagined the East. Capable of courtesy, faith and ferocity.
- **Silhouette at 60 px:** Immediate. Muzzle, ears, maybe a tail. Keep Human-like stature so they do not become "the pet race."
- **Move / jump:** Human-like, with a tracker identity that is probably an ability (scent, night, or "mark a hunted tile") rather than raw MOV, so it does not steal Peri's hop.
- **Race-locked flavours:** hunter; hound-knight or paladin (the Christopher reading); outrider.
- **Setting fit:** The most "a crusader would write this in a letter home" option on the list. Ties to Prester John without inventing a lost tribe of Israel.
- **Sensitivity:** Do not default to Mandeville's cannibal savages, and do not code them as a stand-in for any real people. The Christopher tradition is the safer tone: persons, not beasts.

### Apkallu

- **Origin:** Mesopotamian fish-cloaked sages; also a bird-headed winged type, which we should not use (Peri and, if chosen, Shedu already cover wings).
- **Fantasy:** River sages who wear the fish as a mantle of office. They remember a flood, or they claim to.
- **Silhouette at 60 px:** Unique. A fish head crowning a human one, the skin hanging down the back like a cope. Readable from behind, which most hoods are not.
- **Move / jump:** Cheap water, marsh and ford tiles; awkward on steep dry rock.
- **Race-locked flavours:** sage; purifier; cantor or flood-warden.
- **Setting fit:** Tigris–Euphrates wisdom traditions in a mythic key. Pairs cleanly with holy ground as purification, not as chivalry.
- **Sensitivity:** Same as Shedu: ancient, not a living church. Do not turn the fish-cloak into a comic costume.

### Shir (working name; lion-folk, also Asad)

- **Origin:** Persian *shir*, the lions of Assyrian palaces, and the heraldic lion already in crusader and Islamic art. Not "a lost African tribe."
- **Fantasy:** Beast-blooded melee people with a mane and a pounce. Fierce, formal, easily offended.
- **Silhouette at 60 px:** Mane, muzzle, digitigrade legs, a tail. Mid-to-large, but slimmer than Shedu.
- **Move / jump:** High MOV, a horizontal pounce (distance, not Peri's height hop). Perhaps weaker under heavy magic, as a trade-off.
- **Race-locked flavours:** duellist; holy champion; berserker.
- **Setting fit:** Lions are already the shared emblem of this region and this period (Persian, Assyrian, heraldic, Lionheart). The look is period, not a modern cat-person import.
- **Sensitivity:** Keep the design heraldic and Near Eastern. Avoid tribal pastiche, "noble savage" copy, and generic furry-catalogue faces.

### Vishap

- **Origin:** Armenian storm-and-lake serpent-dragons, a closer-to-home cousin of the naga.
- **Fantasy:** Serpent-bodied highland people of water and weather. They coil where others plant their feet.
- **Silhouette at 60 px:** The strongest body-plan on the list: no legs, a coil, a human or crested torso. Must be drawn so the coil still reads as occupying one cell.
- **Move / jump:** Little or no JUMP. Ignores some rough or water costs; poor at walls and battlements. A hard, interesting trade.
- **Race-locked flavours:** coil-guardian; storm-caller; river mystic.
- **Setting fit:** Armenian highlands, lakes, and storm stones. Silk Road without leading with a sacred Hindu or Buddhist naga.
- **Sensitivity:** Prefer this name and tradition over naga/garuda for that reason. The coil must still look like a person, not a monster add-on, or Q14 gets answered by accident.

### Simurghi (working name; Saena-kin)

- **Origin:** The Persian Simurgh, a healing and protecting bird, sometimes a composite of dog, lion and peacock.
- **Fantasy:** Tall, fully feathered bird-folk: messengers and menders, not sprites.
- **Silhouette at 60 px:** Taller than Humans, a raptor or crested head, full wings. Very clear, and very close to Peri if the wings are the first thing you see. Differentiate with size and plumage, or do not pair them with Peri.
- **Move / jump:** A real glide or a high JUMP, which is a different verb from Peri's short hop but still competes for the "air" niche.
- **Race-locked flavours:** sky warden; healer (the Simurgh as all-healing tree-bird); messenger.
- **Setting fit:** Excellent on paper, same cultural neighbourhood as Peri. That is also the problem: two Iranian winged peoples in a four-race game.
- **Sensitivity:** Folklore and court art, not a current cult image in the way Garuda can be. Still, do not copy sacred temple forms from living faiths under a new name.

## 4. Recommendation

**Recommended four:** Humans, Peri, **Shedu**, and **Ifriti**.

That is a clean triangle of roles around the Human generalist: Peri is small, quick, and hops; Shedu is large, heavy, and holds a gate; Ifriti are the magical, elemental middle, with horns and fire instead of wings. All three non-Human traditions sit in the same Near Eastern and Silk Road neighbourhood as Outremer, and Shedu in particular have a natural conversation with holy and profane ground (they are threshold wardens). Visually they should not collide if Shedu wings stay architectural and heavy, Peri wings stay faint and small, and Ifriti have no wings at all. The risk to watch in art is the Ifriti silhouette: if the horns and ember hair are timid, they will look like Humans. That is a brief for Guilhem, not a reason to drop them.

**Alternate four:** Humans, Peri, **Kaji**, and **Cynocephali**.

This is the less standard-fantasy pairing, and it may be the better one if Ifriti feel too close to "genie" or Shedu feel too winged. Kaji give you a stocky mountain-and-craft people with a real Caucasian address, and they can carry the magic niche as storm-smiths. Cynocephali give you the single best crusader-marvel silhouette and a Prester John hook that does not lean on the Bible. Neither has wings, so Peri keeps the air to herself. The trade-off is that you lose a dedicated fire-mage look and a monumental guardian look; Kaji must be allowed real magic and real mass, not dwarf-comedy, or the quartet leans too far toward "cute Peri, dog-knights, and smiths."

**Runners-up, if a pick above falls:** Shir if you want a beast-mobile pounce and a heraldic lion instead of cynocephali; Apkallu if you want a sage race with a fish-cope that no one will mistake for a Human; Vishap if you want the boldest body plan and are willing to solve one-cell coils in the 64×68 budget. I would not add Simurghi alongside Peri.

Story origins, how they meet the clan, and whether any of them can fall are left for Q12 and Q14. Once two partners for Humans and Peri are chosen, Guilhem can do concept silhouettes at the real 60 px budget before any names or lore are treated as final.
