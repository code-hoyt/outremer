# How tactics RPGs build their world maps

*Research report, 2026-11-23. Page: [How tactics RPGs build their world maps](https://cursor.com/canvas/store-77423492-0240-4ade-b23c-c31d2a9643f0/4b1991c2-f4e2-4bfc-809c-d820429048d9). Applied 2026-11-23: Option A for the slice, Option B from Chapter 2 (D-00210). Applied 2026-11-23: Chapter 1's multi-part mission is a 2-node quest that unlocks a hidden node (D-00211), not the report's 3-node pitch. Findings unchanged; see [README.md](README.md) for status.*

A reference for Outremer: how FFTA, FFTA2 and nine other tactics games structure the map between battles, written before we pick Chapter 1's multi-part mission. Facts I couldn't confirm are flagged.

## The short version
Almost every tactics game here uses a node map: you tap a place, travel there, and fight or shop. The differences are in what fills the gaps. FFTA lets you build the map yourself. FFTA2 fixes the map but layers a calendar, seasons, regions and auctions on top. Unicorn Overlord swaps nodes for a walkable world where winning a battle frees a town and changes the land around it.

For Outremer, the best match is an FFTA2-style fixed node map with consecration as the layer that changes it, kept small for Chapter 1. Pitches and trade-offs are at the end.

## FFTA (GBA)
- **Map structure:** an empty map of 30 slots joined by paths. After story missions you win a location symbol (town, plain, forest, mountain, desert, marsh, cave or jagd) and place it in any open slot. Four locations are always fixed: Giza Plains, Cyril, Bervenia Palace and Ambervale. ([FF Wiki](https://finalfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Ivalice_(Tactics_Advance)), [GameFAQs](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/gba/560436-final-fantasy-tactics-advance/faqs/33337))
- **Missions:** taken at town pubs. Battle missions name an area you must walk to and enter before a deadline. Dispatch missions send one unit away for a number of days instead of fighting. ([GameFAQs](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/gba/560436-final-fantasy-tactics-advance/faqs/25622))
- **Travel and time:** each step on the map is one day. The calendar runs 5 months of 20 days, each month tied to a race. ([FF Wiki](https://finalfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Ivalice_(Tactics_Advance)))
- **Laws:** judges enforce laws that change every day; you can preview the coming days from the map. Breaking a law gets a yellow card, and a red card if it KOs someone. Law cards add or cancel laws. Jagds are lawless slums, the only places where a KO'd unit can die for good. ([FF Wiki](https://finalfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Law_system_(Tactics_Advance)))
- **Roaming clans:** after an early story mission, rival clans appear on the map and wander for about six days; walking into one starts a fight. Freed areas can come under attack, giving a turf-defense battle with a deadline. ([GameFAQs](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/gba/560436-final-fantasy-tactics-advance/faqs/62342))
- **Revisiting:** roaming clan fights, turf defense and repeatable missions give AP and gil. Treasure hunts pay out when matching tile types touch the same slot (for example 3 towns around one slot gives a Rubber Suit). ([GameFAQs](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/gba/560436-final-fantasy-tactics-advance/faqs/25622), [Eyes on FF](http://home.eyesonff.com/content.php/604-FFTA-Treasure-Hunts))
- **Replay variety:** the world itself differs per run, since tile placement is up to you. The wiki says there are potentially thousands of layouts.
- **Phone fit:** very good. One small screen, a cursor and a single tap per destination.

> **Flagged:** the limit of three roaming clans and three turf attacks at once, and the 30 to 40 day turf-defense window, come from one guide author who says they are unconfirmed. Whether the month changes recruit race is disputed (see [the recruiting report](2026-11-23-ffta-recruiting.md)).

## FFTA2 (DS)
- **Map structure:** a fixed region, Jylland: two continents split into five territories (Camoa, Graszton, Moorabella, Fluorgis, Goug). Two levels: a world map of areas, and an area map inside each with towns and landmarks. ([FF Wiki](https://finalfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Jylland), [manual](https://www.gamesdatabase.org/Media/SYSTEM/Nintendo_DS/Manual/formated/Final_Fantasy_Tactics_A2_-_Grimoire_Of_The_Rift_-_2007_-_Square_Enix.pdf))
- **Missions:** quests from town pubs, split into main quests and sub-quests. One detail screen lists the quest's destination or destinations and how many units can go. Many quests can be dispatched instead of fought. Clan trials are special quests paid in clan points that earn titles and clan privileges. ([manual](https://www.gamesdatabase.org/Media/SYSTEM/Nintendo_DS/Manual/formated/Final_Fantasy_Tactics_A2_-_Grimoire_Of_The_Rift_-_2007_-_Square_Enix.pdf))
- **Travel and time:** crossing one area takes a day; finishing or failing a quest also takes a day; an airship trip between continents takes a day. Resting at two specific spots skips a whole month (useful for waiting on month-locked content). ([gameguide.jp calendar](https://www.gameguide.jp/ffta2/overview/game/%E6%9A%A6%E3%83%BB%E3%82%AB%E3%83%AC%E3%83%B3%E3%83%80%E3%83%BC/))
- **Seasons and months:** 12 months of 20 days in four seasons. The month changes which quests the pubs post, which recruits you meet and which monsters appear where. ([FF Wiki](https://finalfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Jylland), manual)
- **Regions and control:** after an early story quest, each big town runs a yearly auction for its areas. Winning an area gives a 20% shop discount there and halves pub fees. Win enough and you become region master, after which auctions there pay out rare items instead. ([FF Wiki](https://finalfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/Auction_(Tactics_A2)), [s-rpg-navi](https://s-rpg-navi.com/ffta2/entry424.html))
- **Laws and privileges:** a judge watches every battle. Keeping the law keeps your clan privilege (a clan-wide buff you pick before battle) and earns a law bonus; breaking it loses both and blocks revives. (manual, [GameFAQs](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/ds/937330-final-fantasy-tactics-a2-grimoire-of-the-rift/faqs/53370))
- **Hidden areas:** the map marks locations that stay off-limits until conditions are met. A Japanese guide lists unlocks per location, for example a set of garden locations opened by the Nameless Stone quest chain and a hideout opened by a magazine-interview quest. ([gameguide.jp world map](https://www.gameguide.jp/ffta2/overview/world/))
- **Revisiting:** enemy groups and would-be recruits can appear on area maps; you touch them to fight or talk. Encounter levels track the clan's level per one walkthrough.
- **Replay variety:** recruits tied to month and area, auctions, and a large quest pool. The map itself never changes.
- **Phone fit:** very good. Built for a small touch screen: tap an area, tap again to move.

> **Flagged:** the English names of the hidden-area quests come from a Japanese guide and are my translations. How encounters scale comes from a single walkthrough.

## Other tactics games

### Final Fantasy Tactics
- Map: a fixed node map of towns and battle sites joined by paths, across 4 chapters. ([FF Wiki](https://finalfantasy.fandom.com/wiki/World_map))
- Missions: story battles appear on the map in order; towns have shops, the pub (errands) and recruits.
- Travel: walking through a green node can trigger a random battle there; story nodes are fixed fights. ([Neoseeker](https://www.neoseeker.com/finalfantasytactics/faqs/41187-final-fantasy-tactics-battle-list.html))
- Revisiting and grinding: random battles on green nodes, plus the Deep Dungeon late in the game.
- Replay: low; the plot is linear and variety comes from job builds.
- Phone: The War of the Lions shipped on iPhone in 2011 and on Android later, with touch controls. ([Square Enix](https://press.na.square-enix.com/FINAL-FANTASY-TACTICS-THE-WAR-OF-THE-LIONS-NOW-AVAILABLE-ON-ANDROID))

### Tactics Ogre and Reborn
- Map: a fixed node map of Valeria; the story moves you between chapters.
- Branching: an early choice splits Law and Chaos routes, with a Neutral path off it; each route has different battles and recruits. ([DualShockers](https://www.dualshockers.com/tactics-ogre-reborn-law-chaos-neutral-route-guide/))
- World Tarot (added in the PSP version): after the ending you can jump back to branch points and play other routes, keeping your army. ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/tactics-ogre-reborn-review/))
- Reborn removed random map encounters; optional training battles and a level cap that rises with the story replace grinding. ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/tactics-ogre-reborn-review/), [Square Enix blog](https://www.square-enix-games.com/en_US/news/tactics-ogre-reborn-new-features))
- Repeatable side dungeons stay for loot and recruits.

### Fire Emblem
- Most entries are linear chapters with a prep screen; there is no map game.
- The Sacred Stones adds a world map you move on freely, with shops, monster skirmishes that can appear on locations, and the optional Tower of Valni whose floors open as the story advances. ([FE Wiki: world map](https://fireemblemwiki.org/wiki/World_map), [skirmish](https://fireemblemwiki.org/wiki/Skirmish), [Tower of Valni](https://fireemblemwiki.org/wiki/Tower_of_Valni))
- Its story still flags the next chapter, and it splits into Eirika and Ephraim routes.
- Three Houses swaps the map for a monastery hub on a calendar: lessons in the week, a free day to explore, fight, take a seminar or rest, and a story battle each month. ([FE Wiki: Garreg Mach](https://fireemblemwiki.org/wiki/Garreg_Mach_Monastery), [calendar](https://fireemblemwiki.org/wiki/Calendar))
- Replay comes from the house you pick, which sets the route.

### Disgaea
- A hub castle replaces the map; a gatekeeper sends you to stages, and any cleared stage can be replayed.
- The Item World is a randomly generated dungeon inside an item; every 10th floor has a boss, and the layout changes each time. ([StrategyWiki](https://strategywiki.org/wiki/Disgaea:_Hour_of_Darkness/Item_World))
- No travel time and no calendar; the replay and grinding engine is the Item World.

### Triangle Strategy
- No free travel map; chapters move you between places, and you explore towns on foot between battles.
- Exploration gathers information that you use to persuade allies in Scales of Conviction votes, which pick the route. ([Game Rant](https://gamerant.com/triangle-strategy-scales-of-conviction-mechanic-explained/))
- An encampment hub has shops, upgrades and Mental Mock Battles for optional grinding. ([LadiesGamers](https://ladiesgamers.com/triangle-strategy-review/))
- Replay comes from branching routes, several endings and New Game Plus.

### Fell Seal
- An FFT-style node map of story nodes.
- Once a story node is cleared, you can choose Patrol there to fight semi-random battles as often as you like, each node with its own enemy level range. ([Neoseeker](https://www.neoseeker.com/fell-seal-arbiters-mark/walkthrough/Patrols_and_Early_Builds))
- Grinding becomes a button you press, not a random interruption.

### Into the Breach and The Banner Saga
- Into the Breach: each run crosses 4 islands of 8 regions; you pick which island to take on and which missions to do, losing the regions you skip. The final island opens after two islands. ([GameFAQs](https://gamefaqs.gamespot.com/pc/205477-into-the-breach/faqs/76363/islands))
- Its phone port redesigned every window for touch, which shows a mission-select map can work well on a phone. ([The Verge](https://www.theverge.com/2022/6/23/23180572/into-the-breach-netflix-mobile-ios-android-advanced-edition))
- The Banner Saga: your caravan walks a set route day by day; each day burns supplies, and morale and renown are traded against each other. ([wiki: gameplay](https://bannersaga.fandom.com/wiki/Gameplay), [caravan](https://bannersaga.fandom.com/wiki/Caravan))
- Both use time pressure to make the route choice matter more than the map's size.

### Unicorn Overlord
- One walkable overworld split into five nations and smaller regions; battles happen on the map itself. ([Destructoid](https://www.destructoid.com/reviews/review-unicorn-overlord/))
- Liberation quests free towns and forts. Freed towns open shops, and the roaming enemies around them disappear. ([official site](https://unicorn-overlord.com/system/explore/))
- Ruined towns are rebuilt with materials you gather, adding inns and other services; you can station one ally there to earn a reward after each battle. ([official site](https://unicorn-overlord.com/system/explore/), [Game8](https://game8.co/articles/latest/976))
- Forts host mock battles and unit expansion, paid with Honors and gated by Renown rank.
- Losing a roaming-enemy fight sends you back to liberated ground.
- Reviews praise the loop of free, rebuild and prepare, but [RPG Site](https://www.rpgsite.net/review/15687-unicorn-overlord-review) notes the empire never fights back to retake land, and Destructoid found regional side quests repetitive.
- The arcs can be played somewhat out of order, which RPG Site says leaves companions from other arcs feeling absent.

**Closest parallel:** Unicorn Overlord's liberation is the nearest thing to consecration: winning a place changes it, makes the land around it safer and opens services. Its main gaps are a lack of pushback and a heavy walking map.

## What this means for Outremer
What we already have: an FFTA2-style node world map with towns for missions and shops ([DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md)), multi-part missions that lock travel back to town (M7 in [SLICE-PLAN.md](../SLICE-PLAN.md)), consecration starting at the end of Act 1 ([STORY.md](../STORY.md)), and FFTA2-style recruiting.

### Takeaways
1. **Keep a node map with tap-to-travel.** FFTA, FFTA2, FFT's phone port and Into the Breach's touch redesign all show it works on small screens, and it is what M7 already plans.
2. **Give consecration a visible, local payoff.** FFTA2's auctions give a 20% discount where you win, and Unicorn Overlord's liberation opens shops and clears roaming enemies nearby. A holy node should change how its corner of the map plays.
3. **Let the profane push back, but later.** FFTA's turf-defense battles give held land a reason to matter, and Unicorn Overlord was criticized for having none. A profane counterattack on a holy node fits Chapter 2 or later, not the slice.
4. **Pick one grinding valve.** The choices are random roaming fights (FFTA, FFT), a Patrol button on cleared nodes (Fell Seal) or optional training (Tactics Ogre: Reborn). A Patrol-style button is the most phone-friendly and never interrupts travel.
5. **Use days, but keep seasons for later.** FFTA2 ties recruits and quests to month and area, which suits our generic recruits. Our time-sensitive branches close at the finale, so they are chapter-based and need no calendar. For the slice, count days and leave seasons out.
6. **Treat multi-part missions as a route and hidden nodes as rewards.** FFTA2 quests list several destinations, which matches our rule that a multi-part mission can't return to town between legs. One side mission revealing a hidden node, as FFTA2 does, is a cheap way to make Chapter 1's map feel bigger.

### Option A: FFTA2 regions with a consecration layer
A fixed, hand-made node map split into regions, with a town in each. Travel is one day per node. After Act 1, profane nodes can be won and consecrated, unlocking that node's fixed offering (shop, mercenaries, upgrade or saint summon).
- Pros: already matches DESIGN.md and the M6 and M7 plans; tap-to-travel nodes fit 390 px easily; story geography stays where the writing needs it; smallest data and art load.
- Cons: replay variety comes only from consecration order and recruits; less customization than Cletus usually wants.

### Option B: fixed nodes, chosen blessings
The same fixed map, but when you consecrate a node you choose what it becomes from 2 or 3 options, such as a chapel (saint summons), market (shop), barracks (mercenaries) or forge (upgrades). Neighboring holy nodes could combine for a bonus, the way FFTA's tiles do.
- Pros: customization and trade-offs each run; gets FFTA's build-your-world feel without moving story locations; each playthrough's holy ground looks different.
- Cons: more balancing and UI work; adjacency bonuses can feel like a spreadsheet; not needed until consecration starts after Chapter 1.

### Option C: a walkable overworld (Unicorn Overlord-lite)
A continuous scrolling map where you walk your party, see profane enemies roaming, and watch the land change color as it turns holy.
- Pros: the strongest feel of holy ground spreading; roaming enemies and hidden places are easy to show.
- Cons: big art and engineering cost, beyond slice scope; walking and camera control are harder on a phone; reviews flag repetition and a lack of pushback.

### Recommendation (not locked)
Build Option A for the slice, and shape the data so Option B can sit on top from Chapter 2. Skip Option C. For Chapter 1, that means one small region where the hunters work, with one town, a few wild nodes and the stronghold. The multi-part mission runs across 3 neighboring nodes, and winning the finale consecrates the stronghold as the first holy node, teaching the map game right as it begins.

One possible Chapter 1 shape (placeholders only, not story decisions): crash site (rescue fight) → hunters' town (missions, shop) → the wilds (side missions); town → leg 1 → leg 2 → leg 3 (multi-part, no return to town); town → holy stronghold (finale, first holy node).
