# Outremer: character briefs

Working briefs for the story cast, written so Guilhem can start drawing the real people and so later writing stays consistent. Earlier test characters (Renaud, Yusuf, Wren) are set aside and should not be used as a base for anyone here.

## How to use this

- **Story facts** follow [STORY.md](STORY.md). Anything marked LOCKED there is canon. Anything marked DRAFT or still listed under open questions is tentative; this file will say so.
- **Visual notes are proposals**, not decisions. Appearance has not been locked by Cletus. Every look, colour, age range, and piece of gear in a Visual brief is **Proposed**, for Guilhem to explore and for Cletus to pick from. Do not treat a proposed detail as canon in art, code, or later story writing.
- **This file gets updated as decisions lock.** When a look or a fact is chosen, move it out of Proposed and point back to the date and source.
- Sample lines are **examples of voice**, not locked dialogue, unless a line is already quoted as canon in STORY.md.

Combat classes are noted only where the story bible already names a role. The full class system has not been designed yet (see DESIGN.md) and is not invented here.

## Sprite and period constraints (locked art rules, open looks)

These constraints apply to every visual brief. They come from the art handoff and style guide, not from the story.

- Battle units are about **48 art px tall** on 48x24 art-px tiles (48x56 cells). At that size, give each character **one or two strong identifiers** (a hood, a cross, a lamp, a bulk and a dark mantle). Anything smaller than about 2 px will read as noise.
- Sprites: **eyes and brow only, no mouth**. Expression, age detail, and mouths belong in portraits.
- Named units get **open faces** (coif, kettle hat, nasal helm). Full helms are for generics only.
- **No weapon at idle.** Identity has to live on the body (silhouette, hair, clothes, a token). Weapons appear in attack frames and in portraits.
- One saturated **identity colour** per unit; the rest muted and earthy. Templar white should be cream, not pure white. A Templar's red cross sits on the **tabard front only**.
- Current sprite pipeline (for later drawing, not a look lock): a shared face-and-body base, distinguished by hair and clothes, in the squire-ref4 style direction. Avoid inventing a different proportion system per hero.
- **Period:** keep the real-world people plausible for the late 12th to 13th-century Crusader states. The exact year is still an open story question. The other world may be more mythic, but it should still feel medieval rather than modern-fantasy.
- Real-world heroes should look as if they belong in our world. Natives of the other world should look as if they belong there.

## The real-world heroes

Only these four, plus Guérin and his cult, come from the real world.

### Tristan

Young Templar knight and protagonist.

**Story role (canon)**
Tristan is a younger son of minor nobility who took the vows because the family had nothing else to give him. He is sincere about the Order, and quietly unsure about God: sceptical, not bitter, because he has never seen anything supernatural himself.

- **Act 1:** At Guérin de Montclair's castle he overrules Lucien and leads the squad into the cellars. The veil tears and he is pulled through with a cultist, who is killed by a Fiend. Dismas's hunters rescue him. He joins them, learns the other world, and finds Aveline at the holy stronghold during the first true Demon attack.
- **Act 2:** Aveline tells him that she, Godric and Lucien followed him through and were later separated. The party takes Eighth Day jobs, finds Godric facing Vaskar, and sees a shadowy figure pull Vaskar out.
- **Act 3:** The figure is revealed as Lucien. When Aveline breaks, Tristan steps up (midpoint of his faith arc and of the romance). He sees Guérin and Simeon in communion through a tear, then holds his faith on the retreat after Vaskar's death and the fall of an Eighth Day stronghold.
- **Act 4:** He helps retake land as holy ground spreads, meets Lucien in the later encounters, and fights Simeon, then Sulthrane. After Sulthrane falls, the former sceptic walks into the collapsing tear and prays. That prayer consecrates the last profane ground, the map turns holy, and the dying gate becomes the road home. This is the first time he speaks scripture. An epilogue will say where he ended up; that text is not written yet.

**Personality and voice**
Measured, plain-spoken, and more sure of his duty than of his faith. He does not preach, and he does **not quote scripture until the final prayer**. He listens more than he speeches. He is kind without being soft, and he will overrule a friend when the work demands it.

Example lines (not locked):

- "I've never seen a miracle, brother. That doesn't mean I won't keep the Rule."
- "The baron's welcome was warm. The cellars still need looking at."
- "Stay behind me. We go together, or not at all."

The verse he speaks at the end is not chosen yet. Candidates for the closing tone are John 1:5 and Revelation 21:5; the exact line is still open.

**Relationships**
- **Aveline:** love interest. The bond grows over the story; he steps in when she can no longer hold things together. How far the romance goes (and what the epilogue does with it) is open.
- **Godric:** loyal friend. Tristan once stood up for him; Godric has not forgotten.
- **Lucien:** brother-in-arms, and Aveline's younger brother. Tristan overrules him at the castle, which plants the slight the cult will use. He wants Lucien back, and does not close that door.
- **Dismas:** first guide in the other world, and a living proof that someone can return from the profane side.
- **Guérin:** received as a gracious host in Act 1; the truth comes later.

**Combat role (canon, class system still open)**
Sword-and-shield Templar. A frontliner and solid all-rounder. Do not invent a job tree for him here.

**Visual brief (Proposed — not canon)**

- **Silhouette:** a young knight in a cream tabard, readable even in a cluster of Templars because he is slighter and cleaner-lined than the sergeants and generics around him. Shield-and-coif shape; no great helm.
- **Build and age:** lean-athletic, not bulky. Early to mid 20s.
- **Palette:** cream tabard, red cross, mid-brown mail and leather. Identity colour is the Templar red on cream, kept clearly brighter than Godric's dark sergeant kit. Hair a dark brown.
- **Gear and props:** mail with a coif or a simple nasal helm worn back or off in dialogue; a plain heater or late kite shield, unheralded (minor nobility, younger son). Sword is his weapon, shown in attacks and portraits, not at idle. A short, close-trimmed beard is the more period-plausible option (Templar brothers were expected to wear them); a clean-shaven face is the option if we want him younger on a phone. Either is Proposed.
- **At 48 px:** (1) the cream tabard with a clear red cross on the chest; (2) a slighter, younger stance than Godric. The cross must still read on the 3/4 front view.
- **Open:** beard or no beard; exact shield shape; how (or whether) Eighth Day marks appear on him after Act 1.

### Aveline

Battlefield medic who grows into a Saint-Caller. Love interest. Lucien's older sister.

**Story role (canon)**
She served in the Hospital tending wounded soldiers, and she is the one in her family who always holds everything together. She starts as a kind, approachable medic; in the other world she learns to call on saints. She is the steadily faithful one for the whole game, so Tristan's growth is visible against her constancy.

- **Act 1:** She sees Tristan fall into the tear and follows with Godric and Lucien. They are later separated. The party finds her at the holy stronghold, calling for aid against the first true Demon, already using blessed help as a Saint-Caller. Meeting her ends the act.
- **Act 2:** She tells Tristan what happened: they fought in a battle, Lucien was taken, Godric went after him, and she has heard from neither since. She had already joined the Order of the Eighth Day, and the party takes its jobs. At the climax she may see something familiar in the shadowy figure and refuse to believe it.
- **Act 3:** The unmasking of Lucien is the moment she breaks. Tristan steps up. After the reveal she begins pleading with Lucien to come home, and she never stops, right to the end. In the working encounter draft, she later heals him when he is left wounded (draft, not locked).
- **Act 4:** She keeps asking him back through the last stand and the finale. She does not take Tristan's place in the final prayer; the point is that we have watched her faith all game, and now we see how far his has come. Epilogue whereabouts are unwritten.

**Personality and voice**
Warm, practical, and steadier than she looks. She holds people together until she can't, and even then she does not close her heart. She quotes **KJV scripture naturally** in ordinary speech, as someone who lives with it, not as a sermon. She is never cruel to Lucien, and she never gives up on him (locked).

Example lines (not locked):

- "The Lord is my strength and my shield. Yours too, even when you doubt it." (Psalm 28:7, in passing, while binding a wound)
- "I will not leave you comfortless. That was a promise, Lucien. It still is." (John 14:18)
- "Tristan fell, and we went after him. That is all. We do not leave our own."

**Relationships**
- **Tristan:** love interest; she trusts him, and he becomes the person she can fall apart in front of.
- **Lucien:** her younger brother. His betrayal hits her hardest, and his return pays off for both of them. She is always open to him.
- **Godric:** trusted friend from the same squad; she is anxious for him through Act 2.
- Saints are a relationship of prayer, not a costume: she asks, and help is given. That is the foil to Simeon's counterfeit miracles.

**Combat role (canon, class system still open)**
Starts as a battlefield medic and grows into a Saint-Caller: a healer who can call on saints in the other world. Do not design the summon list or a class tree here.

**Visual brief (Proposed — not canon)**

- **Silhouette:** an unarmoured field-hospital figure among knights: a mid-length gown or kirtle, a simple headcloth, and a satchel worn at the hip. She must not be mistaken for a Templar knight, and she should not be drawn as a modern fantasy mage.
- **Build and age:** slight to average, mid 20s, clearly older than Lucien and of an age with Tristan.
- **Palette:** dusty blue-grey as her identity colour, with cream linen and brown leather. A small white cross (Hospital, not the Templar red) may sit on a dark mantle if we want her Hospital service to read at a glance; that cross is optional and Proposed, because her exact standing with the Hospital is not spelled out.
- **Gear and props:** medical satchel, linen headcloth or light wimple (open face, hair partly visible so she and Lucien can share a family colour in portraits). No heavy mail. Saint-Calling should be a light or a presence in the cast animation and in portraits, not a new battle outfit, unless a later costume pass is chosen.
- **At 48 px:** (1) the headcloth plus the satchel, or the headcloth plus the dusty-blue gown; (2) a clearly unarmoured, narrower silhouette against Tristan and Godric.
- **Open:** whether she wears a Hospital white cross; how much hair shows; whether Eighth Day gives her a later over-garment once that Order's look is designed. She and Lucien should share a hair colour in portraits (proposed: dark auburn) so the siblings read as family.

### Godric

Hot-headed loyal friend. Common sergeant.

**Story role (canon)**
An English or Saxon-born common sergeant, quick to anger and fiercely loyal to Tristan, who once stood up for him. He holds strong when it counts.

- **Act 1:** He is with the squad at Guérin's castle, sees Tristan fall, and follows with Aveline and Lucien. After a later battle, Lucien is taken and Godric goes after him. He is not seen again until the end of Act 2.
- **Act 2:** The party runs into him already facing Vaskar and teams up with him. Vaskar is beaten, then pulled away by the shadowy figure. Godric might almost recognise the way the figure moves, but he does not get a clear look.
- **Acts 3–4:** He is with the party for the unmasking, the low point, the campaign, and the finale. He does not die. Epilogue whereabouts are unwritten.

**Personality and voice**
Blunt, loud when provoked, and simple in his loyalties. He charges first and thinks second, except when a friend is on the line, and then he is immovable. He is not a fool; he just has little patience for talk that delays a fight.

Example lines (not locked):

- "He was my brother before he was theirs. We go and get him."
- "I don't care what the baron said. Tristan said we look, so we look."
- "That wasn't a stranger's footwork. I know what I saw."

**Relationships**
- **Tristan:** the friend he would follow through the veil, and already has.
- **Aveline and Lucien:** squad family. Lucien's turn is a betrayal of that, not only of Aveline.
- **Vaskar:** the man he is found fighting; they have no older history unless one is written later.

**Combat role (canon, class system still open)**
Heavy hitter: a big axe or a mace (which one is not decided). High damage, charges over defence.

**Visual brief (Proposed — not canon)**

- **Silhouette:** the broadest of the four heroes. A dark sergeant's mantle and a heavy, planted stance. He should be readable as "the big one" even as a black shape.
- **Build and age:** bulky, late 20s to early 30s, a head shorter or simply thicker than a tall knight, with the weight in the shoulders and chest.
- **Palette:** black or dark brown mantle (the historical sergeant's colour, and a clear contrast with Tristan's cream), with a red cross on the dark ground. Identity colour is that dark russet-brown, not the same bright cream. Fair or sandy hair and a fuller beard, to mark the English or Saxon origin without a caption.
- **Gear and props:** mail under the dark mantle; coif or kettle hat, face open. The axe or mace is his signature in attacks and portraits, not at idle, so the bulk and dark mantle have to carry the sprite. A thick belt and heavy boots help the weight read.
- **At 48 px:** (1) dark mantle against Tristan's cream; (2) the broader, heavier body. Hair colour is the backup cue, not the main one.
- **Open:** English vs Saxon as a flavour note (it changes little on a sprite, more in voice and portrait); axe vs mace.

### Lucien (and the Hooded Figure)

Aveline's younger brother. The prodigal son. Skirmisher and scout.

**Story role (canon, with one working-draft stretch)**
Bright, ambitious, and resentful of being overlooked. The cult uses that resentment. He looks up to Guérin, and at the castle he argues that the squad should respect the baron's word. Tristan overrules him. That is the seed of his turn.

- **Act 1:** He crosses with Aveline and Godric, is later taken in a battle, and is recruited by Vaskar with the same "they'll never value you" pitch Vaskar once used on Dismas.
- **Act 2 (working draft, not loved yet; the rescue itself is locked):** mid-act, a Hooded Figure is seen at Vaskar's side; the figure leads a weapons-smuggling run, fights the party, and escapes; the figure then guards one of Simeon's false miracles and hesitates when it is exposed; at the climax, the figure pulls the beaten Vaskar out. Clues should echo things the player already saw Lucien do in the prologue (a phrase, a fighting move, a token). Godric may half-recognise the movement; Aveline may see something familiar and refuse to believe it. The betrayal is not confirmed in this act.
- **Act 3:** The hood comes off at Simeon's rite. Lucien is there with Vaskar. Aveline breaks. He also hears, through the tear, that the war is an invasion of his own home. He does not come back yet, but he does not finish the party (a missed killing blow, or a path left open). He is present when Dismas kills Vaskar and fails to stop it.
- **Act 4:** A few encounters pull him back from the edge so the return feels earned. In the working draft those include the wounded-brother scene (Aveline heals him; he slips away and leaves something from home) and a last stand where he guards the way to Simeon and lets the party pass, while she is still asking him to come home. Exact scenes are still to be written, and the run is marked for revisit. In the final battle he returns by killing Simeon, too late to stop Sulthrane being summoned. The kill still matters: it stops Simeon finishing the rite as its master. Lucien does not die. Epilogue whereabouts are unwritten.

Aveline's pleas start after the reveal, and she never gives up (locked).

**Personality and voice**
Quick, sharp, and hungry to be taken seriously. He is not cruel at heart; he is proud, and he nurses slights. Early on he sounds like a young man who has learned court manners and likes them. After the turn he is colder, then raw once the doubt starts. As the Hooded Figure he should say very little.

Example lines (not locked):

- "The baron has been nothing but courteous. We ought to take him at his word."
- "You will always be their second thought. I am offering you the first." (Vaskar's pitch, which Lucien comes to believe)
- "This was meant to be a war of faith. Not a door into our own house."

**Relationships**
- **Aveline:** his older sister, who never stops asking him home.
- **Tristan:** fellow knight in the detachment, and the man who overrules him in front of Guérin.
- **Guérin:** the lord he admires, whose charm makes the cult's side look honourable at first.
- **Vaskar:** recruiter and, for a time, the person who seems to value him. Vaskar's death shakes him.
- **Simeon / Sulthrane:** the false faith he serves until he turns on Simeon.
- **Dismas:** a mirror he may not recognise at first: a man who already came back, and who could not save his own friend.

**Combat role (canon, class system still open)**
Skirmisher and scout: fast and clever, with a bow or twin blades. Which weapon is not decided. Whatever is chosen should be the same weapon the Hooded Figure uses, so the prologue can rhyme with the Act 2 fights.

**Visual brief (Proposed — not canon)**

Lucien needs two readable looks that are clearly the same body: the young scout of the prologue, and the Hooded Figure of Acts 2–3.

*Lucien, unhooded*

- **Silhouette:** lean and light-footed, shorter or narrower than Tristan, in scout leather and a short cloak rather than a knight's tabard. (Whether he is a vowed Templar, a sergeant, or an attached scout is not stated; he is part of the Templar detachment. Do not put him in a full white knight's mantle unless that is chosen later.)
- **Build and age:** slim, late teens to early 20s, clearly younger than Aveline.
- **Palette:** dull amber or russet short cloak as his identity colour, with brown leather and muted greens. Dark auburn hair, shared with Aveline.
- **Gear and props:** light kit, open face, maybe a short coif or no helm. A small personal token from the prologue (a belt ring, a wrist wrap, a clasp) that can return as a missable clue on the Hooded Figure and as the thing he leaves behind after Aveline heals him. The token is Proposed; the idea that some token or habit should echo the prologue is from the story draft.

*The Hooded Figure*

- **Silhouette:** the same lean body under a deep hood and a longer concealing cloak. The hood is the identifier. It must hide hair and face enough that the player cannot be sure, without changing his height or way of moving.
- **Palette:** charcoal or ash cloak, dark enough to read as "the enemy's scout," not so black that he vanishes on dark tiles. A hint of Lucien's amber at a lining, hem, or the hidden token is the missable tell.
- **At 48 px:** as Lucien, (1) the russet short cloak and lean stance; as the Figure, (1) the deep hood. The second cue in both looks is the same lean outline, so a player who is paying attention can feel the rhyme. Weapons do not have to do this work at idle.
- **Open:** bow vs twin blades; his exact status in the Order; the hooded cloak's cut; whether the hood is worn down after the unmasking or only in the Figure scenes.

## Other-world natives

Dismas, Vaskar, Simeon, Sulthrane, the Eighth Day, and the hunters are of the other world. They should not look like they just stepped out of a Crusader castle.

### Dismas

Converted former cultist. Leader of the hunting party that rescues Tristan. Named after the penitent thief.

**Story role (canon)**
A native of the other world who once served the profane side with his closest friend, a sworn brother in spirit but not by blood. When Dismas turned to the light he had to "betray" that friend. The friend stayed, rose to become Simeon's lieutenant, and is now Vaskar. Dismas cannot save him, only end him. He is living proof that someone can come back, which mirrors and foreshadows Lucien's return.

- **Act 1:** His party arrives as the Fiend turns on Tristan. He fights beside him, is a little puzzled by how confused Tristan is, gives him a crash course, and takes him on for a few minor quests. The hunters work with Eighth Day as scouts.
- **Act 2:** With the party through the Eighth Day jobs and the clash with Vaskar. He does not get to finish that fight.
- **Act 3:** He kills Vaskar at the low point, which completes his beat. A trusted hunter or an Eighth Day captain may carry any extra cost of the lost battle; see the gap noted at the end of this file.
- **Act 4:** Still with the party for the campaign and the finale (one of the later Lucien encounters may be his; that is only a recommendation in STORY.md). He does not die. Epilogue whereabouts are unwritten.

**Personality and voice**
Weathered, practical, and harder on himself than on other people. He has already made the worst choice and the right one, and he does not boast about either. He is kind in a blunt way, and genuinely puzzled that Tristan treats the supernatural as a shock.

Example lines (not locked):

- "You fight well for a man who keeps asking whether the enemy is real."
- "I came back. That doesn't mean everyone does. Don't make me your proof until you've seen the cost."
- "I can't bring him with me. I can only stop him."

**Relationships**
- **Vaskar:** the friend he lost and must finally end. This is the wound that defines him.
- **Tristan:** first charge and, later, a comrade. Dismas is the guide; Tristan becomes a peer.
- **Lucien:** the man Dismas's story exists to foreshadow. How much they talk before the return is still open.
- **Eighth Day:** he works with the Order as a scout, and is not described as a brother of it.

**Combat role**
Not classed in the story bible. He is a hunter and the leader of a hunting party. Treat him as a skirmish or light-frontliner until the class system is designed. Do not invent a kit here.

**Visual brief (Proposed — not canon)**

- **Silhouette:** a holy hunter, not a Templar: a worn mid-length coat or cloak, a distinct un-Frankish cut, and (in attacks and portraits) a hunting spear, short blade, or lantern-staff. A small lantern at the belt is a strong identity prop even if weapons stay off the idle sprite.
- **Build and age:** rangy rather than bulky, mid 30s to 40, older than the four heroes and visibly lived-in.
- **Palette:** ash, bone, and a faded gold as his identity colour (penitent light, not Simeon's bright lamp-gold). No cream tabard, no Templar cross. Hair dark going grey.
- **Gear and props:** open face, a short beard or heavy stubble, a simple other-world holy mark rather than a Crusader cross. A scar, brand, or old cult mark he does not bother to hide would tell the conversion story in a portrait. Any shared token with Vaskar (a clasp, a braided cord) will help their history read.
- **At 48 px:** (1) the lantern, or the ash cloak with the faded-gold mark; (2) a leaner, more travel-worn outline than the real-world knights. He must not be mistakable for Godric or for an Eighth Day regular once that look exists.
- **Open:** exact weapon; whether he wears any Eighth Day sign; the form of the old cult mark.

### Vaskar

Simeon's lieutenant. Dismas's lost friend. Lucien's recruiter.

**Story role (canon)**
Once Dismas's closest friend, a sworn brother in spirit, not by blood. He stayed on the profane side, rose under Simeon, and is so far gone into Sulthrane's power that he is barely a man any more. He recruits Lucien with the same "they'll never value you" pitch he once used on Dismas.

- **Act 1:** No glimpse. He is saved for Act 2.
- **Act 2:** Mid-act, the Hooded Figure is seen at his side (working draft). At the climax he fights Godric, then the party; he is defeated, and the Hooded Figure pulls him out.
- **Act 3:** He is with Lucien at Simeon's rite. Dismas kills him at the low point. Lucien is there and fails to stop it. That is the end of him; he is not saved.

**Personality and voice**
What is left of the man is a recruiter's warmth over something hollow. He is persuasive when he wants a soul, and barely human when he does not. Keep enough of a person in his early lines that Dismas's grief makes sense, and let the later fights show how little of that person remains.

Example lines (not locked):

- "They'll never value you. I told an old friend that once. I was right then, too."
- "Dismas chose a clean conscience. I chose a side that wins."
- Later fights can go thinner than that: short, wrong, almost not a voice.

**Relationships**
- **Dismas:** the friend he will not follow back, and the man who ends him.
- **Lucien:** the next recruit, used the same way, and the figure who saves him at the end of Act 2.
- **Simeon and Sulthrane:** master and the power wearing him down.

**Combat role**
Simeon's lieutenant. A mid-to-late game human-plus boss, faced in Act 2 and killed in Act 3. No class is named. He should feel tougher than a Fiend and less than a true Demon or Simeon, but that tuning is not designed yet.

**Visual brief (Proposed — not canon)**

- **Silhouette:** taller than Dismas, still the shape of a man, with armour or cloth that has started to fuse or warp. He must remain recognisable as a person, or the tragedy flattens into a monster fight. A remnant of the same hunter cut as Dismas, corrupted, would do that work.
- **Build and age:** once the same generation as Dismas (mid 30s to 40s), now stretched or thinned, as if something is wearing him.
- **Palette:** cold violet or sickly gold as the identity colour, against ashen skin and darkened metal. Avoid Simeon's clean ivory, and avoid making him look like Sulthrane in miniature; he is a man being spent, not the demon itself.
- **Gear and props:** open, ruined face (named unit): too-hollow eyes, still human. A cracked version of the shared token with Dismas. Lieutenant's marks in Simeon's false-light style, worn wrong.
- **At 48 px:** (1) the warped, taller lieutenant silhouette; (2) the sickly gold or violet against Dismas's faded gold. The human face has to read as a face, not a skull, or he will be mistaken for a generic Hollow or Fallen.
- **Open:** how far the "barely a man" corruption goes in Act 2 vs Act 3 (a later, worse pass is an option); exact colours; weapon.

## Villains

### Guérin de Montclair

The Lord. A charming, well-liked baron in the real world, secretly working for the cult. The "clair" (bright) in his name is ironic.

**Story role (canon)**
Lucien looks up to him. He is not fought as a boss. His soul is bound to Simeon's (unique to these two, not a rule of the world). When Simeon dies, Guérin dies with him, and the player should see that through the tear.

- **Act 1:** The squad is sent to his castle after a frightened chaplain writes to the Templars behind his back. By the time they arrive, the chaplain has "gone to visit his sister"; his body is later found in the cellars. Guérin is a warm, charming, dismissive host, and he quietly steers them away from the lower keep. Players should read this as pride or negligence, not villainy, so the Act 3 reveal still lands. A servant warns the squad, and they inspect anyway.
- **Acts 2–3:** He is offstage in the real world until the Act 3 glimpse: through a tear, in communion with Simeon. That is when the bound souls and the plan come out: the opening tear was only the first step, and they mean to take the real world for Sulthrane. Lucien hears it too. This is a glimpse, not a return.
- **Act 4:** He dies in the same instant Lucien kills Simeon, seen through the tear. Both faces of the villain fall at once.

Canon line, already in STORY.md:

- "Rumours, brothers. Peasants see devils in every shadow. But you're welcome to look, after supper."

**Personality and voice**
Gracious, unhurried, and hard to dislike. He flatters without obvious oily-ness. He sounds like a man who has always been obeyed and has learned to make it feel like hospitality. He should still seem plausible as a generous host on a first meeting.

Further example lines (not locked):

- "You've ridden far, and on a fool's errand, I fear. Still, my house is yours."
- "The young man has a courtly head on his shoulders. You might hear him now and then, brother Tristan."

**Relationships**
- **Lucien:** the youth he charms, and whose admiration he uses.
- **Simeon:** bound soul, partner in the plan, the other face of the same work.
- **The chaplain and the household:** people he can remove or ignore. The servant who warns the squad is braver than the house deserves.
- **Sulthrane:** the power the plan is for; Guérin may not show that loyalty in public.

**Combat role**
None as a boss. He may never need a full battle sprite if he appears only in the castle and in tear-glimpses, but a sprite and a portrait should still be designed, in case a glimpse or a flashback is staged on the board.

**Visual brief (Proposed — not canon)**

- **Silhouette:** a secular baron, not a knight of the Order: a longer tunic and a pale mantle, court-length, with a simple device. In the castle he should look richer and cleaner than the travel-worn Templars.
- **Build and age:** handsome, well-kept, 40s to early 50s, a little taller than Tristan, not a warrior-brute.
- **Palette:** pale gold and cream as the ironic "bright" identity, with clean mid-blues or soft greys. No demonic marks on the Act 1 clothes. Do not put a lamp on his arms; that would telegraph the Simeon link too early. A plain white mountain or pale peak (a quiet nod to "Montclair") is enough, and even that is Proposed.
- **Gear and props:** open face, neatly trimmed beard or a clean shave, a lord's clasp, no great helm, no Templar cross. The Act 3 glimpse can show the same man under stranger light, not a new costume, so the player recognises him at once.
- **At 48 px (and in portraits, which may do more work than a sprite):** (1) the pale, too-clean mantle; (2) the baronial cut, clearly not a Templar tabard.
- **Open:** the exact device; beard; whether he is ever seen in mail.

### Simeon the Lamp

The Prophet. A false miracle-worker already in the other world. A false light, and a dark mirror of Aveline's saints.

**Story role (canon)**
His "miracles" are demonic. The theme is true faith against counterfeit faith. He is always the opponent, never a false ally. He twists scripture, quoting it out of context the way the devil quotes Psalm 91 in Matthew 4. He is Sulthrane's servant; Sulthrane gave him his powers, motivations and goals. Through the bound soul with Guérin, he knows the real world and the war. The cult's tear in the opening is step one of his invasion. He is not listed among the people who come from the real world, so he should be treated as a native of the other world, though STORY.md never says that in one direct sentence.

- **Act 1:** He is not met. His work is already under way.
- **Act 2:** The party comes into contact with his forces and, in the working draft, with a public "miracle" that is a Fiend wearing a saint's light.
- **Act 3:** The party catches him at a rite, opening or widening a tear. Lucien is there with Vaskar. The communion with Guérin is seen through that tear. The rite still succeeds enough to matter.
- **Act 4:** The party fights and defeats him, but he is not yet dead. He tries to summon Sulthrane. Lucien kills him. It is too late: the gate is already open. Guérin dies with him. Sulthrane arrives.

**Personality and voice**
Serene, certain, and wrong. He sounds like a holy man who has decided that light belongs to him. He uses scripture as a tool, not as a rule he is under. He should be able to say something that almost sounds true.

Example lines (not locked):

- "He shall give his angels charge over thee. Did you think that promise was only for you?" (Psalm 91, twisted, as in Matthew 4)
- "I am sent to give light to them that sit in darkness. Stand aside, and see it done." (echoing Luke 1:79, taken for himself)
- "Your saint comes when she is asked. Mine comes when I call. Which of us is heard?"

**Relationships**
- **Guérin:** the other face; the means by which he knows and reaches the real world.
- **Sulthrane:** his master, whom he tries to bring through at the end.
- **Vaskar and Lucien:** instruments. He does not need to love them.
- **Aveline:** thematic foil, even when they are not speaking: she asks the saints; he counterfeits them.

**Combat role**
The last human (or once-human) boss, fought in the Act 4 finale before Sulthrane. No class is named. His kit should feel like false miracles: light that harms, blessings that are not blessings. Do not design the encounter here.

**Visual brief (Proposed — not canon)**

- **Silhouette:** tall and robed, with a lamp as the must-read prop (a staff-lamp, or a hanging lamp he carries). At a glance he should look sacred; on a second look, too still, too clean, a little wrong. He is a dark mirror of Aveline, so her asked-for light and his held lamp should read as cousins, not as the same effect.
- **Build and age:** long and narrow, ageless or late middle age, taller than Guérin, not burly.
- **Palette:** ivory and bright, almost-holy gold as the identity colour, with a sick or ashen undertone in the shadows of the robe. This gold should be cleaner and harsher than Dismas's faded gold, and not the same as Sulthrane's.
- **Gear and props:** the lamp, always. Open, serene face (portraits carry the wrongness: a smile that does not reach the eyes, or eyes that hold the lamp-light). No Templar or Hospital cross. Scripture or false-sigils on the robe are optional and easy to overdo; the lamp is enough.
- **At 48 px:** (1) the lamp; (2) the tall ivory robe. If the lamp is lost at that size, the character is lost.
- **Open:** lamp as staff vs hand-lamp; how human he still looks by Act 4; whether the rite and the boss fight use the same robe.

### Sulthrane

The demon above all others. An invented name. The final boss.

**Story role (canon)**
Sulthrane gave Simeon his powers, motivations and goals. Simeon is his servant. The invasion of the real world is for him. He is reserved above Fiends and (named) Demons; facing a Demon is already a big deal, and he sits over that.

- He is not met in Acts 1–3, except as a name and as the power behind Vaskar and Simeon.
- **Act 4:** After Simeon is defeated, Simeon tries to summon him. Lucien kills Simeon, but the gate is already open and Sulthrane comes through. He is defeated with saints and angels at the heroes' side. When he falls, the tear starts collapsing, and Tristan's prayer turns that collapse into the way home.

His own voice, private motives beyond taking the real world, and any earlier glimpses are not written yet.

**Personality and voice**
Open. He can be a presence more than a talker, or a last, plain claim on the world. If he speaks, keep it short and grave, not witty, and do not let him out-talk Simeon's false scripture; the last mask coming off should feel older than that.

Example direction (not locked, not lines): a claim, a dismissal of the saints, or silence under a look that fills the board.

**Relationships**
- **Simeon:** servant and door.
- **Guérin:** the real-world hand, known through that bond.
- **Vaskar:** a soul already nearly spent.
- **The saints and angels:** the host that stands with the party in the last fight.

**Combat role**
Final boss. A step above the Act 1 true Demon (whose name is still TBD). Fought with saints and angels present. Encounter design is not done.

**Visual brief (Proposed — not canon)**

- **Silhouette:** a broken crown, a torn veil, or a vast winged mass that still reads as one figure. Prefer a medieval sense of a fallen power (hybrid, kingly, wrong) over a modern red devil. He should not look like a larger Vaskar or a darker Simeon; those were masks and servants.
- **Scale:** Proposed that he may break the 48 px unit height, as a boss, so long as the board can still read him on a phone. That scale exception is not locked and needs an art and engine pass.
- **Build and age:** not human-aged. If he has a body, it should feel borrowed or unveiled, not simply tall-and-muscled.
- **Palette:** deep dark with a counterfeit gold that echoes Simeon's lamp and then outgoes it. The gold is a lie; the dark is the read. Avoid a generic fire-red unless Cletus chooses it.
- **Gear and props:** a crown, a ruined halo, or the torn veil of the world, used as one mass, not as tiny ornaments. No need for many small symbols.
- **At 48 px, or at boss scale on a phone:** (1) size plus the crown/veil/wing mass; (2) the dark-and-false-gold colour. If he is only "a big demon," he will not feel like the last reveal.
- **Open:** almost everything. This is the least decided look in the cast, and should be explored as a set of options, not drawn once and assumed.

## Others to define later

Named or implied roles that do not have briefs yet. Looks, names, and voices here are unset, except where STORY.md already gives a fact.

- **The castle chaplain.** Wrote to the Templars behind Guérin's back about Satanists. When the squad arrives he has "gone to visit his sister." His body is later found in the cellars. A real-world man; probably only needs a portrait and a corpse, not a battle kit.
- **The servant who warns them.** Slips the squad a warning so they inspect the lower keep anyway. Real-world, unnamed, courage only, no other facts.
- **Dismas's hunters.** The hunting party that rescues Tristan and becomes his first team. Natives of the other world, scouts working with Eighth Day. Individuals are undefined. A trusted hunter is mentioned as a possible bearer of the Act 3 "extra cost."
- **Eighth Day figures.** The Order of the Eighth Day is the native holy order, a spiritual counterpart to the Templars. It has a holy stronghold as a house, posts jobs, and is already opposing Simeon. Aveline (and the others, before they were separated) joined it; Tristan's clan then becomes part of it, or at least takes its missions. An Eighth Day captain is another possible bearer of the Act 3 cost. No grand master, no uniform, and no named brothers or sisters are set. Their look should be designed as a group before individual briefs.
- **The Act 1 true Demon** (name TBD). Attacks the holy stronghold in the Act 1 finale ("Saint-Caller Calling for Aid"). The first true Demon the player faces, a step up from the opening Fiend, and a big deal for that reason. Needs a name, a look, and a fight identity of its own, distinct from later Demons and from Sulthrane.

Lesser enemy kinds (Imps, Wretches, Blights, Hollows, Gheists, the Fallen, Fiends, and later Demons) are bestiary terms, not characters, and are out of scope here.
